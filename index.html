MobileVISTA: Generative Data Augmentation
for Pose Generalization in Mobile Manipulation

Anonymous Author(s)

Paper ID 947




Abstract

Mobile manipulators such as humanoid robots are increasingly deployed in dynamic, unstructured environments to perform dexterous manipulation tasks. However, end-to-end manipulation policies trained to imitate demonstration data collected from a single robot pose are brittle: even centimeter-scale deviations in robot pose at deployment can drive egocentric observations and end-effector trajectories out of the training distribution, leading to sharp drops in performance. At the same time, collecting additional real-world data with diverse poses can be prohibitively expensive. We introduce MobileVISTA, a data generation framework that transforms demonstrations captured at canonical poses into diverse, pose-perturbed training data by jointly augmenting egocentric visual observations and retargeting actions to compensate for base pose changes. Policies trained on this augmented data learn to respond to previously out-of-distribution poses encountered at test time. To evaluate its effectiveness, we study MobileVISTA in both simulated manipulation tasks and on a real Galaxea R1 Pro mobile manipulator. Across domains, MobileVISTA improves robustness to unseen base perturbations, while requiring no additional real-world demonstration collection. These results highlight MobileVISTA as a practical step toward deploying mobile manipulation systems that can reliably chain navigation and manipulation skills.

Method Overview

MobileVISTA takes manipulation demonstrations collected from a single canonical robot pose and generates training data covering a wide range of base poses, without collecting any additional demonstrations. For each augmented trajectory, we sample a base perturbation and jointly synthesize two things: a pose-shifted egocentric observation (via geometry-based reprojection, robot segmentation, and inpainting) and a retargeted action sequence (via inverse kinematics in end-effector space). Policies trained on the resulting dataset learn to handle base poses far outside the original demonstration distribution at test time.

Real-World Deployment

We deploy MobileVISTA on a Galaxea R1 Pro humanoid and compare against three baselines: a vanilla 2D diffusion policy trained only on canonical-pose demonstrations (Canonical-2D), a 3D point-cloud-based diffusion policy (DP3), and a fine-tuned π0.5 vision-language-action model. For each evaluation trial, we sample a base offset uniformly within a 15cm disk and manually drive the robot to that offset, then roll out all four policies from the same starting configuration in randomized order. Quality Analysis of Synthesized Observations

We compare MobileVISTA's geometry-based observation synthesis against GEN3C, a state-of-the-art learned novel view synthesis model. Across all four simulated tasks, MobileVISTA produces sharper object boundaries, more consistent scene geometry, and higher PSNR against simulator-rendered oracle views. Policies trained on MobileVISTA-synthesized data also outperform those trained on GEN3C-synthesized data across the full range of perturbation magnitudes, with the gap in downstream performance tracking the gap in reconstruction quality.


Results

We evaluate MobileVISTA across four simulated tasks spanning two embodiments: Can Sort and Pouring on a Fourier GR-1 humanoid, and Box and Drawer on a bimanual Franka setup. MobileVISTA consistently outperforms Canonical-2D, DP3, and fine-tuned π0.5 baselines across all tasks, maintaining high success rates as the magnitude of base perturbation grows. The advantage holds even at large 15cm offsets, where the baselines collapse to near zero.
